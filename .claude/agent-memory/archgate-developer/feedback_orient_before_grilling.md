When a grilling round happens inside a **long-running effort** — a wayfinder map, a phased ticket,
anything spanning sessions — open with two or three lines of orientation before the first `❓`:
which map and ticket this is, what the previous phase landed, and what this phase must produce.
Only then present the questions.

**Why:** Han ran `/wait-what` on a phase-2 grilling round (2026-08-28) — "Wait, I don't understand
where you've got to here." The round itself was fine: evidence table, three questions, a stated
recommendation each. What was missing was the frame. He had invoked `/wayfinder #4` and received
questions about entry-point globs and `eslint-plugin-boundaries` with no restatement of what the
map was for or what phase 2 owed. The evidence was all there and none of it was anchored. After the
re-pitch he answered all three in one line ("Q1: B, Q2: B, Q3: Ok"), so the questions were never the
problem.

**How to apply:** Any grilling round where the reader has not been in the thread continuously — the
first round of a wayfinder session, a round after a long tool sequence, a round resuming
cross-session work. Cheap insurance: a "Where we are" heading costs four lines and removes the only
failure mode that wastes a whole round. Keep the round format itself unchanged — see
[[grilling-format-prose-not-picker]]. If he asks for `/wait-what`, the fix is orientation and plainer
language, not fewer questions or weaker recommendations. See [[han-operator-author]].
